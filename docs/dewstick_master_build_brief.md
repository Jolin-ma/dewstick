# Dewstick — Waitlist Site Master Build Brief

**Prepared:** August 30, 2026
**Status:** Draft for review — see "Open decisions" before build starts

---

## 1. What we're building and why

Dewstick is a tinted lip balm positioned as half lipstick, half lip mask: real color, plus squalane, shea butter, and ceramides doing the work a $30+ luxury lip mask would do. The product and packaging are already designed (see section 3). What doesn't exist yet is anywhere to send people.

This build is not a store. It's a single waitlist landing page whose only job is to get an email address. The point is to find out, cheaply, whether people who see the product actually want it, before any money goes into inventory. Everything in this brief should be judged against that one goal: does it help someone decide to hand over their email in the next 15 seconds on their phone.

## 2. A strategic flag before we go further

The reference site, tuttilipstick.com, isn't just a style reference here, it's a very close analog. Tutti sells the same idea (lipstick that acts like a lip balm), the same ingredient trio (squalane, ceramides, plus their own moisture story), a similar cloud/blob pattern on the tubes, and a similar bold rounded wordmark. Dewstick's existing assets already lean into a different angle that's worth pushing harder: the "luxury lip mask, in a stick" line and the "asmr, the sound, the texture, the dew" macro shot both point toward a skincare-first, slightly more elevated read than Tutti's playful $16-a-tube energy.

Worth deciding now, not after launch: does Dewstick want to sit right next to Tutti (same lane, same price point, competing on shade range and packaging cuteness) or does it want to own the "this is a lip treatment first, color is the bonus" position more fully. The copy in this brief leans toward the second option because it's the angle the existing assets already support best, but this is a call for you to make deliberately rather than one the site design should make by accident.

## 3. Brand snapshot — what already exists

Pulled from the 12 images in your Pictures/dewstick folder. This is a real, finished-looking asset set, not a sketch, so the brief below is written to use it directly rather than invent a new look.

**Wordmark and colors:** bold, rounded, slightly bubbly red sans-serif wordmark ("dewstick"), reading almost identically in weight and playfulness to Tutti's lettering. Packaging uses a white cloud/blob pattern over five candy colorways: red, a warm orange/mango, lime green, soft pink, and sky blue. Backgrounds across the shots rotate through cream, blush pink, powder blue, and warm tan. Accent script/handwriting shows up for callouts ("get-ready with me," "so cute!," "100% you").

**Product:** a lipstick-format tube (twist-up bullet, not a squeeze balm) in five shade-coded colorways, sold as singles or as a five-pack gift box. Box copy: "happy shades happy days." 4g / 0.14 oz, labeled "tinted lip balm."

**Ingredient story, already illustrated with icons:** squalane (deep hydration), shea butter (soft & smooth), ceramides (strengthen & protect). A separate icon set frames the same idea as a benefit list: hydrate, nourish, protect, glow.

**Existing lines worth reusing verbatim or near-verbatim:**
- "half lipstick. half lip balm."
- "all the care. all the color."
- "one swipe. juicy glow."
- "a luxury lip mask — in a stick."
- "100% you"
- descriptor tags: juicy, glossy, balmy
- "asmr — the sound. the texture. the dew."
- checklist style: hydrating, tinted, effortless

**Gaps in the existing set:** no shade names yet (tubes are color-coded but the actual shade/tint names aren't established), no price point stated anywhere, no logo file separate from packaging mockups, no real customer/UGC photography (image 7 is a posed model shot, not organic UGC), no video or GIF asset for the texture/ASMR angle even though the concept already exists in a still image.

## 4. Reference analysis: what to take from Tutti, what to leave

Take: the confident, one-line hero claim; ingredient benefits shown as short icon-and-label rows rather than paragraphs; a milk-splash/cloud pattern as a recurring texture rather than a one-off graphic; playful sticker-style callouts; a simple, low-friction email modal with a one-line privacy reassurance.

Leave or adapt: Tutti's copy voice is very casual and joke-forward ("the joke is the lid"). Dewstick's existing assets are warmer and a little more polished, closer to a skincare brand that happens to be fun, so the copy in this brief pulls back from full joke-voice and keeps things warm and simple instead. Also skip Tutti's per-shade e-commerce grid entirely, since there's no store yet, in favor of one hero shade lineup shot and a single, unmissable email capture.

## 5. Who this page is for

Someone who already spends on skincare-adjacent beauty (lip masks, sleeping masks, treatment balms in the $18 to $35 range) and would consider a tinted version if it didn't feel like a compromise. They're on a phone, probably arriving from an Instagram or TikTok post, and they've never heard of Dewstick before this exact moment. The page has to explain the whole idea in one screen's worth of scrolling before asking for anything.

## 6. Page goal and how we'll know it worked

Primary conversion event: an email submitted to the waitlist. Everything else (scroll depth, time on page, social clicks) is secondary. Since this is a validation exercise and not a revenue page, the number that actually matters at the end is simple: out of everyone who lands on the page from a given traffic source, what percentage leaves an email. A mobile landing page in this category doing well typically lands somewhere in the 15 to 30 percent range when the traffic is warm (people who already saw the product on social), so that's the rough bar, not a guarantee.

## 7. Page structure, section by section

The page is one long scroll, single column, mobile-first. Order matters, each section earns the right to the next one.

1. **Hero.** Full-bleed product shot (image 1 or 2 work well here) with the wordmark, the line "half lipstick. half lip balm.", and a short supporting line drawn from the existing assets: "a luxury lip mask, in a stick." Email input and a single button sit directly under the fold, no scrolling required to see them. Button copy: "join the waitlist," not "submit" or "sign up."
2. **The problem/promise.** One short block, no more than two sentences, naming the actual tension the product resolves: you shouldn't have to choose between real color and real lip care. This is the section that turns a pretty picture into a reason to care.
3. **Ingredient proof.** The three-icon row that already exists (squalane, shea butter, ceramides) with the short benefit words already written (deep hydration, soft & smooth, strengthen & protect). Keep this to icons and four-word labels, no ingredient essay.
4. **Texture/sensory moment.** The ASMR macro shot (image 9) full-width, with its existing line. This is the section that does the emotional, tactile selling that ingredient callouts can't.
5. **Shade lineup.** The five-tube lineup shot (image 11), with its existing line "one swipe. juicy glow." This is a teaser, not a shop grid: no prices, no add-to-cart, just "five shades, one formula" and a repeat email capture.
6. **Second capture / social proof placeholder.** Since there's no real UGC or review data yet, this section should be honest rather than faked: something like "be the first to try it" plus a founder-style line about why the waitlist exists (early access, first pick of shades, a note when it's real). Do not fabricate reviews, star ratings, or customer counts to fill this section.
7. **FAQ, short.** Three to five questions max, phone-length answers: what is it, when does it launch, will there be a discount for waitlist members, how will my email be used.
8. **Final capture and footer.** One last, low-pressure ask ("still deciding? we'll just say hi when it's ready") plus minimal footer: contact email, Instagram/TikTok handle if set, privacy line.

## 10. Mobile conversion checklist

The email input and button must be visible without scrolling on a standard phone screen, in the hero, not just lower on the page. Tap targets on the button need to be large enough to hit with a thumb, no fine print-sized links doing double duty as buttons. Total page weight should stay light: compress every image, since the existing packshots are already 300 to 430kb each and a page with all twelve unresized would load slowly on mobile data. Form should ask for email only, nothing else, every extra field lowers completion. No pop-up interstitials on load. The page should work with one thumb, one hand, no pinch-zooming needed anywhere.

## 11. Waitlist mechanics and email tool

Recommendation: start with Klaviyo's free tier (free up to 250 contacts, which comfortably covers a validation phase). The reasons this beats a bare Google Form: it gives a real double opt-in and confirmation email out of the box, it's the tool most DTC beauty brands eventually run their launch and post-purchase flows on, so there's no migration later, and it plugs into a simple embedded form on a custom-coded page without needing a backend of your own. If zero cost matters more than any of that during early testing, a Google Form linked to a Sheet is a fine fallback, just note that you'd rebuild the capture flow again once the store is real.

## 12. Tech and build plan

Single-page, custom-coded site (HTML/CSS/JS or a lightweight framework), deployed on Vercel, matching the "custom coded page" direction you confirmed. No CMS needed for a one-page waitlist. The email form posts directly to Klaviyo's list API (or the chosen tool's embed/form snippet), no custom backend required. Domain: dewstick.com currently returns an empty/unclear response rather than a clear parked-for-sale or fully live page, worth checking directly with a registrar (or your domain provider if you already hold it) before build, since we can't confirm ownership or availability from here.

## 13. Open decisions needed from you

Positioning lane: sit next to Tutti's playful/value space, or push the "luxury lip mask" angle harder (see section 2). Shade names for the five colorways. Whether there's a launch price or price range to hint at anywhere on the page (even "starting at $--" builds anticipation, but only if you're comfortable committing to a range this early). Whether the five-pack gift box is part of the initial story or held back for launch. Confirmation on the domain. Final call on Klaviyo vs. a simpler free tool for the actual build phase.

## 14. Rough build sequence

Lock positioning and copy from section 13 first, since it changes hero and section 2 wording. Then: source or export a clean logo, compress and crop the five chosen images, build the page section by section in the order in section 7, wire up the email capture to Klaviyo, test on an actual phone (not just a resized browser window) before sharing the link anywhere.
